A. Recognition and circulation
A1. UFCMJRA public policy — § 4(c)(3)
A court need not recognize a foreign-country money judgment if the judgment or the claim for relief is repugnant to the public policy of this state or of the United States. Discretionary. Resisting party bears the burden. Mandatory bars stay in § 4(b) (system due process, PJ, SMJ).
Standard in the comments / cases: difference of law is not enough. Recurring formulas — injury to public health, morals, or confidence in justice; “fundamental notions of what is decent and just.”
1962 Act was narrower (cause of action only; this state only). 2005 added the judgment itself and federal policy so label-of-the-claim games fail.
Not decided here: Hague 7(1)(c); punitive haircut as a dedicated clause (UFCMJRA has none — often parked in public policy).
Working rule: odiousness test, not a merits appeal. Defamation after 2010: lead with the SPEECH Act, not § 4(c)(3).
A2. SPEECH Act — 28 U.S.C. § 4102
Federal bar. Shall not recognize a foreign defamation judgment unless the creditor shows:
(a)(A) foreign law in that case ≥ First Amendment + forum-state law, or
(a)(B) the opponent would have been liable under U.S. / forum-state defamation law; and
(b) foreign personal jurisdiction comported with U.S. due process; and
(c) if the defendant is an ICS, outcome consistent with § 230.
Appearance abroad does not waive. Burden on the creditor. Fees: § 4105. Declaration: § 4104. Removal: § 4103.
Case: Trout Point Lodge, Ltd. v. Handshoe, 729 F.3d 481 (5th Cir. 2013). Nova Scotia default; Canadian truth-burden ≠ U.S. falsity-burden; (A) failed; (B) failed because the default/pleadings did not show U.S. liability (no adequate falsity). First appellate construction of the Act.
Not decided in Trout Point: § 4102(b) in depth; § 230 / (c) (Handshoe was the speaker); every Canadian judgment forever.
Working rule: foreign libel default is a SPEECH Act case first. Creditor must try a U.S. defamation case in all but name. UFCMJRA public policy is backup.
A3. Hague texts (U.S. not a party)
2019 Judgments Convention
Front door: civil/commercial judgment of a Contracting State + Art. 5/6 filter.
Art. 7 may refuse: (a) notice, (b) fraud, (c) public policy — “manifestly incompatible” (case-level fairness + security/sovereignty), (d) contrary to a forum/trust clause, (e)–(f) inconsistent judgments. 7(2) pending review.
Art. 10: may refuse the non-compensatory / punitive slice; keep compensation; count costs/fees toward compensation. Do not dump ordinary damages disagreement into 7(1)(c).
Official report: Garcimartín–Saumier.
Public policy letter is 7(1)(c), not (d).
2005 Choice of Court
Front door: international, civil/commercial, exclusive clause naming a Contracting State court, not on Art. 2 list (consumers, employment, many IP/immovable carve-outs).
Art. 5 chosen court hears; Art. 6 others stand down; Art. 8/9 circulate / refuse.
Art. 7 is interim measures, not refusals. Refusals = Art. 9. Punitive slice = Art. 11.
Exclusive by default unless parties say otherwise.
Official report: Hartley–Dogauchi.
U.S. signed, not ratified.
Working rule: exclusive Contracting-State clause → ask 2005 first. Else 2019 filters. Else national law (UFCMJRA / SPEECH Act in a U.S. court today).

B. Section 230
B1. The statute, two immunities
(c)(1) — ICS or user not treated as publisher/speaker of information provided by another ICP.
(f)(3) — ICP = responsible in whole or in part for creation or development of that information.
(c)(2) — Good Samaritan: good-faith restriction of objectionable material (and tools for others). Moderation is not a Stratton Oakmont trap.
(e) list (not development):
Federal criminal enforcement
Intellectual property
State law consistent with 230 (inconsistent state claims preempted)
ECPA / similar
FOSTA — see B3
Own words never enter (c)(1). That is threshold, not an (e) exception.
B2. Roommates development — 521 F.3d 1157 (9th Cir. 2008) (en banc)
Development = material contribution to the alleged unlawfulness of particular information. Feature by feature.
Feature
230
Site-written required drop-downs (sex / familial status)
No immunity — ICP of those fields
Match / filter / email on those required fields
No immunity
Open Additional Comments
Immune — Zeran hosting

Test sentence: delete the defendant’s words and required fields. If the remaining user text is still the same tort, the defendant did not develop the unlawfulness.
Not development: hosting, ranking, recommending third-party files; notice-and-failure-to-remove; profit; existence of a comments box.
2012 (666 F.3d 1216): FHA / FEHA do not reach shared-living roommate choice. Roommate lost 230 on the forms and still won the case. Cite 2008 for immunity; 2012 for FHA scope only.
Working rule: scalpel. Site-authored or site-required unlawful content can leave (c)(1). Third-party speech in an open field generally cannot.
B3. Gonzalez v. Google, 598 U.S. 617 (2023) (per curiam)
Non-decision on § 230. Complaint (ATA: YouTube hosted and recommended ISIS videos) appeared to state little claim independent of 230 after Twitter v. Taamneh, 598 U.S. 471 (2023) (general hosting / recommending ≠ ATA aiding-and-abetting). Court declined to address 230.
Ninth Circuit 2021 (2 F.4th 871): recommendations of someone else’s videos were (c)(1); revenue-sharing not barred by 230 but failed to state a claim. SCOTUS reviewed only 230 and then sidestepped it. Revenue-sharing unreviewed.
Working rule: cite Gonzalez only for “SCOTUS will not decide 230 on an empty ATA complaint.” Cite Taamneh for ATA. Cite 2021 Ninth Circuit if you need an actual recommendations-as-(c)(1) holding. Cite Roommates for development.
B4. FOSTA — § 230(e)(5) only
Pub. L. 115-164. There is no FOSTA “section 513.”
230 does not impair:
Civil 18 U.S.C. § 1595
State criminal charges for conduct that would violate § 1591
State criminal charges for conduct that would violate § 2421A (ICS operated with intent to promote or facilitate prostitution of another)
(c)(2)(A) good-faith blocking stays protected. Amendments apply to conduct before or after enactment. Federal crimes were already out under (e)(1).
Not in (e)(5): defamation, ATA, ordinary negligence-to-remove, state civil lookalikes that are not 1595.
After the exception, plaintiff still must win: § 1595 benefit + participation in a venture + knew or should have known the venture trafficked. § 1591(e)(4): participation = knowingly assisting, supporting, or facilitating an (a)(1) act. § 2421A still needs intent.
B5. Doe v. Backpage, 817 F.3d 12 (1st Cir. 2016)
Pre-FOSTA § 230 case. Cert. denied. Civil claims treating Backpage as publisher of user sex ads barred. (e)(1) is not a civil on-ramp. Design/anonymity/moderation rules still publisher conduct. Venture element not reached. This is why Congress wrote (e)(5). It is not the Arizona criminal case and not a 1595 holding.
B6. Post-FOSTA venture case law
Narrow appellate pole — Ninth Circuit
Jane Does 1–6 v. Reddit, 51 F.4th 1137 (9th Cir. 2022): (e)(5)(A) requires the website’s own conduct to violate § 1591. User trafficking alone does not lift 230. Participation = knowing assistance, not “known problem” / weak moderation.
Doe v. Twitter (9th Cir. 2023): follows Reddit. Participation is more than a continuous business relationship with users.
District magnet — Doe v. Kik, 482 F. Supp. 3d 1242 (S.D. Fla. 2020): 230 gate still needs § 1591-quality knowing active participation; constructive “known problem” insufficient.
Hotel / motel 1595 cases: often “should have known” + this room. Physical venture. Poor analogue for a general ICS. Many never face 230.
Backpage criminal (D. Ariz.): 2018 seizure; mixed 2023–24 result (Lacey one laundering count, many hung/acquitted counts; Spear/Brunst 10 years). Not a wall of § 1591 convictions. Civil 1595 / vendor cases often stayed behind that docket.
Working rule: website must be a knowing participant in the trafficking, not a place where trafficking posts appeared. Cite Reddit/Twitter/Kik for apps. Cite hotels only for hotels. Cite Doe v. Backpage only for why (e)(5) exists.

C. Personal jurisdiction
C1. Specific-jurisdiction triangle
       FORUM
       /     \
DEFENDANT —— CLAIM
Defendant formed the forum contacts (Walden v. Fiore, 571 U.S. 277). Plaintiff’s home / felt injury is not a contact.
Claim arises out of or relates to those contacts.
Arise out of = causation.
Relate to = Ford (below).
Not related = Bristol-Myers nonresident with no forum hook.
Fairness factors still exist; rarely eject a true Ford resident case.
Not a corner: national ads alone; other plaintiffs’ local cases; “the app is available here”; size.
C2. Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District, 592 U.S. 351 (2021)
Specific PJ. Ford not at home in MT/MN.
Facts: resident injured in the forum in an Explorer / Crown Victoria. Those models marketed, sold, serviced there. Units first sold elsewhere.
Holding: market for those very vehicles + resident + in-forum injury is related enough. No VIN-level but-for in-state first sale required.
Does not: revive general PJ; allow BMS tag-alongs; decide the internet; decide 230.
C3. Bristol-Myers Squibb v. Superior Court, 582 U.S. 255 (2017)
Specific PJ is per plaintiff. California could hear CA Plavix claims. Not nonresidents who did not get, use, or suffer injury from Plavix in CA. Sliding scale = fake general PJ.
Impact: mass inventories against a non-home defendant fragment. Ford did not undo this for strangers. Fifth Amendment / class-action absent members not decided. Rule 4(k)(1)(A) usually imports BMS in diversity.
Platform: if the ICS is at home in the district, Daimler swallows BMS. If not, one local Doe does not pull the rest.
C4. Daimler AG v. Bauman, 571 U.S. 117 (2014)
General PJ = essentially at home.
Paradigm: incorporation + principal place of business.
Rejected: “substantial, continuous, systematic course of business” as all-purpose PJ.
n.19: exceptional third home — Perkins — operations “so substantial and of such a nature.” Daimler-in-California “plainly” was not. No later SCOTUS Perkins forum.
n.20: appraise the company worldwide. Operate in many places ≠ at home in all of them.
Imputation of MBUSA assumed arguendo; still no.
C5. Perkins v. Benguet Consol. Mining Co., 342 U.S. 437 (1952)
Forum is Ohio, not Oregon.
Philippine miner; Japanese occupation; president ran the company from Ohio (office, directors, files, banks). Surrogate PPB / headquarters in exile. Unrelated stockholder claims. Due Process allows general PJ; remanded for Ohio state-law choice to hear the case.
Not dealers, ads, users, or a large subsidiary. Helicopteros purchases ≠ Perkins.
